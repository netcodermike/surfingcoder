What the tableau is telling you

Phase I finds feasibility. Slack variables convert ≤ constraints to equalities; ≥ constraints need surplus variables. Artificial variables provide a starting basis for ≥ and = rows. Phase I maximizes the negative sum of artificials. A negative optimum means the original model is infeasible.

Phase II improves the objective. This app writes the objective as z − cᵀx = 0. A negative objective-row coefficient signals an improving entering variable. The minimum nonnegative ratio selects a leaving row. Bland’s smallest-index rule resolves ties and avoids exact-arithmetic cycling.

Sensitivity belongs to a basis. Shadow prices measure local objective changes for a constraint RHS; coefficient and RHS ranges change one parameter at a time. Multiple simultaneous changes require re-optimization. At range endpoints there may be alternative optimal bases.

Uncertainty is modeled, not predicted. The expected-weather model maximizes probability-weighted worth for one fixed plan. Optimizing each weather separately assumes perfect foresight; it is a comparison benchmark, not an achievable forecast.

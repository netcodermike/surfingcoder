The WSL Rankings Are Misleading

The published WSL standings heading into Pipeline appear straightforward. Yago Dora leads Gabriel Medina on the men's side, while Molly Picklum leads Carissa Moore on the women's side.

But when I dug into the WSL's best-nine-of-twelve scoring format, a different picture emerged.

The standings show current totals, but they don't show which scores are currently counting, which scores are likely to be dropped, or how much surfers can gain from the weighted Pipeline finale.

That creates situations where two surfers separated by more than a thousand points may actually be much closer than the rankings suggest.

For example, Gabriel Medina appears to benefit more than Yago Dora from the best-nine structure because of the lower scores available for replacement. The same dynamic may exist between Carissa Moore and Molly Picklum in the women's race.

Pipeline's increased points weighting further magnifies these effects.

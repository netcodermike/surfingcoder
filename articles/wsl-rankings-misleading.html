The lead that changes without a heat win

Yago Dora leads Gabriel Medina by 1,740 points heading into Pipeline. Yet if both lose their opening heat, Medina finishes the season ahead. The women’s race has the same twist: Carissa Moore overtakes Molly Picklum if both finish ninth.

The published rankings are correct. They describe the points already counting. What takes a little more work is seeing which score each surfer will replace when the season’s final result arrives. That replacement changes the value of exactly the same Pipeline finish.

Those are comparisons, not automatic championship outcomes: another contender could still pass them. But they explain why subtracting the current gap from 15,000 is an incomplete way to read this race.

Scope: a complete 12-event season, normal competitive participation, and the published advanced seeding. Withdrawals, disciplinary changes, or a revised event format would require a new calculation. The tables below are points conditions applied to feasible results, not heat-by-heat predictions.

A 15,000-point win is not a 15,000-point gain

The 2026 WSL rulebook counts a surfer’s best nine results from twelve events. At this snapshot, the ranking totals already count nine of the eleven completed events. Append Pipeline, discard the three lowest scores, and sum the remaining nine.

Final points = current points + max(0, Pipe points − replacement score)The replacement score is the lowest of the nine results currently counting. If Pipe is lower, Pipe itself is discarded.

Dora’s replacement score is 3,320; Medina’s is 1,000. A men’s ninth at Pipe awards 4,980. Dora gains 1,660, while Medina gains 3,980—a 2,320-point swing, enough to erase the existing gap.

Picklum replaces 4,745, while Moore replaces 2,000. A women’s ninth is worth 3,000. Picklum discards Pipe entirely and stays on 53,695; Moore adds a net 1,000 and reaches 54,150.

Dora, Medina, Ferreira, Colapinto, Pupo and Fioravanti have title paths. Dora and Medina each control their own destiny with a Pipe win. Medina has another advantage: he can still be champion with ninth. Dora needs at least fifth.

Why Ethan Ewing is out

Ewing starts seventh on 41,725 and replaces 1,000. Winning Pipe takes him to 55,725. The top eight men start in Round 4, making ninth their worst normal result. Medina’s ninth produces 57,190, already beyond Ewing’s maximum. Everyone below Ewing is also out under these assumptions.

A tour place can turn on 50 points

The next-season cutoff is the top 22 men and top 14 women. The same best-nine arithmetic determines those places. Missing the postseason does not mean a surfer simply adds all their Pipe points: a new counting score still displaces their lowest counting result.

Our conservative calculation certifies the current top 15 men and top nine women for CT requalification through normal competition. It allows adverse assignments across the available finishing bands. The final draw may protect additional surfers, so these are guaranteed groups—not a claim that everybody below them can actually be eliminated.

Hopkins, Gilmore, and the 50-point difference

If Yolanda Hopkins loses her first heat and finishes 17th, she ends on 27,075. All current top 13 women are necessarily ahead. Hopkins keeps the remaining qualifying spot only if nobody below her passes that total.

Stephanie Gilmore finishing third produces 27,125: just 50 points more. A semifinal for Anat Lelior or Alyssa Spencer also passes Hopkins; Brisa Hennessy, Tya Zebrowski and Vahine Fierro need the final, while Sally Fitzgibbons and Bella Kenworthy need wins. Those results would each displace a first-round-exiting Hopkins.

Hopkins in third reaches 34,700, above every current outsider’s maximum, guaranteeing her place. That is a concrete clinch condition rather than a vague promise that a “deep run” should be enough.

Marshall and Mamiya’s identical schedules

Jake Marshall and Barron Mamiya both start on 19,705 and replace 1,000. They therefore have identical final-points schedules. If both finish 25th, each reaches 20,205 while the current top 21 men remain ahead. A challenger passing that score pushes them below the line; otherwise their mutual tie requires the qualification procedure.

Their regular-season result distributions are identical. Current ranking order alone does not settle that tie. The rulebook’s countback, average-heat-score step and possible accommodation through an available WSL wildcard matter. Challenger Series qualification and discretionary wildcard decisions remain separate routes.

Oscar Berry and Francisca Veselko cannot reach their CT cutoffs through Pipe points. Several other low-ranked surfers still have points-based paths, but entry eligibility and the final draw must be checked before calling those paths achievable.

Check the work

This analysis uses the individual event points for all 35 ranked men and 23 ranked women after the Philippines Pro. Every published total reconciles to the best nine of eleven. Each Pipe outcome was then appended and the best nine recalculated. The official rankings were rechecked against this snapshot while preparing the article.

For requalification, the calculation respects the number of places available in each finishing band and the normal entry-round floors. Those aggregate bounds do not model every matchup. The men’s provisional seed template retrieved for the analysis contains a duplicate seed, so this article avoids presenting it as a settled named draw.
